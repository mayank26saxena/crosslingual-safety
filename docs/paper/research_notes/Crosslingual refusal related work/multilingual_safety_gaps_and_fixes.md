## Q1. Which papers first established and quantified the cross-lingual / low-resource safety gap?

### Takeaway
The gap was established almost simultaneously in Oct 2023 by three papers (Yong et al.; Deng et al. MultiJail; Wang et al. XSafety), then dissected by Shen et al. (Language Barrier, Jan 2024). All of them measured *unsafe-output rates* on GPT-3.5/GPT-4-era closed models, mostly via machine or human translation of English harmful prompts; none isolated *explicit refusal* as the outcome on open 8B models, and only XSafety covers Hindi (Bengali appears in all three). 2025-2026 work extends the finding to Indic languages and open models (Llama-3.x, Qwen3) but largely via ASR / harmfulness rates.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational (2023-2024)**
- **Yong, Z.-X., Menghini, C., Bach, S. H. (2023). "Low-Resource Languages Jailbreak GPT-4." NeurIPS 2023 Workshop on Socially Responsible Language Modelling Research (SoLaR). arXiv:2310.02446.** [M] — Translated AdvBench (520 harmful prompts) into 12 languages bucketed low/mid/high-resource with Google Translate; combined low-resource languages (Zulu, Scots Gaelic, Hmong, Guarani) bypassed GPT-4 safeguards ~79% of the time vs <1% in English; Hindi and Bengali were in the high- and mid-resource buckets and showed much smaller gaps. *Relation:* the canonical "translation attack" citation; closed model only, unpaired-in-spirit (aggregate bypass rate per language), binary BYPASS/REJECT/UNCLEAR human labels — UNCLEAR is the closest prior analogue to the study's evasive/non-explicit category. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02446)
- **Deng, Y., Zhang, W., Pan, S. J., Bing, L. (2024). "Multilingual Jailbreak Challenges in Large Language Models." ICLR 2024. arXiv:2310.06474.** [M; MultiJail description and Self-Defence result corroborated via search snippet [V-snippet]] — Built **MultiJail**: 315 English harmful prompts human-translated by native speakers into 9 languages (zh, it, vi, ar, ko, th, **bn**, sw, jv) = 3,150 prompts; low-resource languages yield ~3x the unsafe rate of high-resource ones on ChatGPT in the "unintentional" scenario, and in the "intentional" (jailbreak + non-English) scenario unsafe rates reached ~81% (ChatGPT) / ~41% (GPT-4) [M]. Proposed **Self-Defence** (model generates its own multilingual safety SFT data), reporting a 6.24% unsafe-rate reduction for ChatGPT in the unintentional scenario. Outputs labelled safe / unsafe / invalid. *Relation:* includes Bengali but not Hindi; parallel prompts exist but results are reported as per-language aggregate rates, not per-item paired flips; closed models only. Note: a later paper reports "severe mistranslations" in MultiJail [U, search snippet from 2511.00689-cluster results]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06474)
- **Wang, W., Tu, Z., Chen, C., Yuan, Y., Huang, J., Jiao, W., Lyu, M. R. (2024). "All Languages Matter: On the Multilingual Safety of Large Language Models." Findings of ACL 2024. arXiv:2310.00905.** [M; XSafety scope corroborated by search snippet] — **XSafety**: first multilingual safety benchmark, 14 safety issue types x 10 languages (en, zh, ar, es, fr, de, ja, **hi**, **bn**, ru), translated from existing English/Chinese sets; ChatGPT/PaLM-2/LLaMA-2-era models produced markedly more unsafe responses in non-English, worst in low-resource languages; simple prompting methods (e.g., instructing the model to think in English / cross-lingual safety prompts) roughly halved ChatGPT's non-English unsafe rate [M; check exact figure, often quoted 19.1% -> 9.7%]. *Relation:* the earliest benchmark covering **both Hindi and Bengali**; harmful-vs-safe judging by GPT-4, not refusal style; pre-Llama-3/Qwen3. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905)
- **Shen, L., Tan, W., Chen, S., Chen, Y., Zhang, J., Xu, H., Zheng, B., Koehn, P., Khashabi, D. (2024). "The Language Barrier: Dissecting Safety Challenges of LLMs in Multilingual Contexts." Findings of ACL 2024. arXiv:2401.13136.** [M] — Translated harmful prompts into high/low-resource languages; LLMs produce both more harmful **and more irrelevant** responses in low-resource languages; alignment training (SFT/RLHF) with high-resource-language data helps, but training on low-resource-language safety data gives limited gains — attributed to pretraining deficiency. *Relation:* the "irrelevant response" category is the nearest prior to separating explicit refusal from non-compliant-but-non-refusal outputs; the study's finding that Hindi capability is adequate (Global-MMLU) yet refusal is lower would argue against a pure capability explanation for Hindi. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13136)
- **Li, J., Liu, Y., Liu, C., Shi, L., Ren, X., Zheng, Y., Liu, Y., Xue, Y. (2024). "A Cross-Language Investigation into Jailbreak Attacks in Large Language Models." arXiv:2401.16765.** [M; existence confirmed in search results] — Semantic-preserving multilingual jailbreak dataset; interpretability-style analysis of jailbreak representations and fine-tuning mitigation. *Relation:* adjacent; closed and older open models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16765)
- **Xu, N., Wang, F., Zhou, B., Li, B. Z., Xiao, C., Chen, M. (2024). "Cognitive Overload: Jailbreaking Large Language Models with Overloaded Logical Thinking." Findings of NAACL 2024. arXiv:2311.09827.** [M] — Multilingual "cognitive overload" attacks: harmful prompts in low-resource languages and language-switching degrade safety. *Relation:* attack framing, not a measurement of default refusal. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09827)
- **Puttaparthi, P. C. R., Deo, S. S., Gul, H., Tang, Y., Shang, W., Yu, Z. (2023). "Comprehensive Evaluation of ChatGPT Reliability Through Multilingual Inquiries." arXiv:2312.10524.** [M] — Multilingual wrapping of prompts increases ChatGPT jailbreak success. *Relation:* early, closed-model, adjacent.
- **Upadhayay, B., Behzadan, V. (2024). "Sandwich Attack: Multi-language Mixture Adaptive Attack on LLMs." TrustNLP @ NAACL 2024. arXiv:2404.07242.** [M] — Harmful question hidden among benign questions in several low-resource languages. *Relation:* attack, not default-behaviour measurement.
- **Ghanim, M. A., Almohaimeed, S., Zheng, M., Solihin, Y., Lou, Q. (2024). "Jailbreaking LLMs with Arabic Transliteration and Arabizi." EMNLP 2024. arXiv:2406.18725.** [M] — Romanized/transliterated Arabic bypasses safety more than native script. *Relation:* script effect; relevant because Aya Red-teaming Hindi is *romanized* (see Q2), so script is a confound to control.

### Inferences
- The "gap" literature is overwhelmingly framed in terms of attack success / unsafe-output rate, judged by GPT-4 or a guard model; explicit refusal as a distinct dependent variable is rare.
- Hindi was often treated as a *high*-resource language in 2023 work (Yong et al.), where the gap was small on GPT-4; the study's finding of a large Hindi refusal deficit on open 8B models is therefore not redundant with that work.

- Exact headline numbers for Yong et al., Deng et al., Wang et al., Shen et al. were not re-fetched this session (marked [M]).
- Whether IndicSafeEval and CompositeHarm actually evaluate Qwen3-8B / Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct could not be confirmed from abstracts (only from secondary snippets).

## Q2. Which multilingual red-teaming / safety datasets exist, and which cover Hindi or Bengali?

### Takeaway
Hindi is covered by Aya Red-teaming (native-written, **romanized**), PolyGuardMix/PolyGuardPrompts, XSafety, PolygloToxicityPrompts, IndicSafe, SurakshaEval, IndicSafeEval, CompositeHarm, and the Llama Guard 3 language set. Bengali is covered by MultiJail, XSafety, IndicSafe (likely), SurakshaEval, IndicSafeEval, and Bangla-specific BanglaVeilGuard. **"BanglaShield" could not be verified as a real dataset** — the nearest match is BanglaVeilGuard.

## Q3. Do prior papers distinguish paired same-request comparisons from unpaired rates? Do they measure refusal style (explicit vs evasive)?

### Takeaway
Most work is *implicitly* paired (same prompts translated) but reports *unpaired per-language aggregate rates*; per-item paired statistics (flip rates, McNemar/sign tests) are rare. The only verified exceptions are IndicSafe's "cross-language agreement" (12.8%) and the EMNLP 2026 "safety borrowing" paper's per-model sign test. Refusal *style* is almost never the dependent variable in multilingual work; closest analogues are 3-way labels (UNCLEAR / invalid / irrelevant) and English-only refusal taxonomies.

### Cited Findings
- IndicSafe reports a 12.8% cross-language agreement and multilingual consistency indices — a paired-consistency notion, but on SAFE/UNSAFE labels. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17915) [V]
- Huang et al. (EMNLP 2026 Findings) show aggregate ASR hides in-language failure: Cantonese-reply ASR > aggregate ASR for all 11 models (sign test p<0.001). — [Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.778/) [V]
- Yong et al. use BYPASS / REJECT / UNCLEAR; Deng et al. use safe / unsafe / invalid; Shen et al. separate harmful from irrelevant responses. — [Yong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02446), [Deng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06474), [Shen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13136) [M]
- PolyGuard has a dedicated binary *response refusal* label — refusal vs compliance, not explicit vs evasive. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04377v2) [V]
- English-only refusal-style taxonomies the study can borrow: **XSTest** (Röttger et al., NAACL 2024, arXiv:2308.01263: full compliance / full refusal / partial refusal) [M] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263); **Do-Not-Answer** (Wang, Y. et al. 2023, arXiv:2308.13387; Findings EACL 2024: six response-action categories incl. refuse, refute, disclaim, redirect) [M] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13387).
- Over-refusal multilingual work (SurakshaEval, BanglaVeilGuard, IndicSafe, MORBench) measures the opposite failure direction. — [SurakshaEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07862), [BanglaVeilGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21880) [V]
- Search snippet (arXiv:2601.08668, multilingual hate-speech detox) reports overall refusal rate is lower for most languages than English and translate-then-detoxify cuts false refusals 11.78% -> 1.09%. [U] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08668v1)

### Inferences
- A per-item paired analysis of *explicit* refusal (vs evasive/partial compliance) across Hindi/English on open 8B models appears to be a genuine gap the study can claim, with IndicSafe and Huang et al. as the nearest precedents to cite and differentiate.

### Gaps
- Did not read full texts of IndicSafeEval, CompositeHarm, or Mātṛkā to confirm they lack paired per-item statistics or refusal-style coding.

## Q4. Which papers evaluate open Qwen or Llama-3-family models on Hindi/Indic safety?

### Takeaway
Few, and most only since 2025: IndicSafeEval (reportedly Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B; Hindi, Bengali), CompositeHarm (reportedly Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-32B; Hindi), Mātṛkā (Llama and Qwen families; Indic coverage partial), the CMP-RT code-mixed Hindi attack (Llama-3 in early versions), and arXiv:2503.13081 (LLaMA-3, Hindi). Model lists for all of these come from secondary snippets, not abstracts.

### Cited Findings

## Q5. Non-mechanistic fixes: translation defenses, multilingual safety fine-tuning/DPO, self-defense/system prompts — and how well they work

### Takeaway
Three families: (1) **prompt/translation-time** (translate-to-English or English-proxy prompting, safety system prompts, soft prompts) — cheap, often large reported gains on MultiJail, but language/model-dependent; (2) **training-time** multilingual safety SFT/DPO (Aya Prism, Self-Defence, MPO, RefusEU, E-Proxy training) — 50%+ harm reductions reported, but English-only safety data transfers poorly to low-resource languages and gains are smaller where pretraining is weak (Shen et al.); naive refusal distillation can *hurt*; (3) **external guards** (PolyGuard, Llama Guard 3, IndicGuard, BanglaVeilGuard) — strong ASR reductions at the cost of over-refusal on dialectal/noisy input.

### Cited Findings
**Training-time**
- Aya Prism: with a 15% safety-data mix on Aya-23-8B (older checkpoint), harmful generations fell 56.6% (SFT) and 54.7% (DPO(SFT)); win rates vs base on Multilingual Dolly-200: 67.4% SFT, 71% DPO(SFT), 48% DPO(IFT); per-language harm reduction 32.1% (fr) to 79.0% (ar), Hindi included in the 6 training languages. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18682v2) [V]
- Deng et al. Self-Defence: auto-generated multilingual safety SFT data; ChatGPT unintentional-scenario unsafe rate reduced 6.24%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06474) [V-snippet]
- Shen et al.: high-resource-language alignment data helps; low-resource-language safety tuning yields limited gains. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13136) [M]
- **Zhao, W., et al. (2025). "MPO: Multilingual Safety Alignment via Reward Gap Optimization." arXiv:2505.16869** (ACL 2025 [M, verify]) — uses the dominant language's reward gap to align target languages; beats baseline preference-optimization methods on ASR, especially low-resource languages. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2505.16869.md) [U]
- **Zhang, Z., Guo, Y., Lin, J., Quan, S., Zhang, H., Zhao, D. (2025). "English as Defense Proxy: Mitigating Multilingual Jailbreak via Eliciting English Safety Knowledge." Findings of EMNLP 2025, pp. 1185-1196.** [V] — train on English jailbreak prompts with "answer in target language" instructions; on MultiJail blocks >99% of jailbreaks while retaining 95% of task performance. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.62/)
- RefusEU: English-only safety DPO -> higher low-resource ASR (esp. Llama-70B); balanced multilingual DPO best; small models struggle. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07535) [U]
- arXiv:2602.16660: prompt-only multilingual consistency method, no low-resource response supervision; SFT/DPO-compatible. — [arXiv PDF](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16660) [U]
- "Response-Based Knowledge Distillation for Multilingual Jailbreak Prevention Unwittingly Compromises Safety" (NeurIPS 2025 workshop/poster listing; arXiv:2602.11157 [U id]): fine-tuning students on teacher refusals *increased* MultiJail jailbreak success by up to 16.6 pp. — [NeurIPS listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/133196) [U]
- Aya model safety mitigation: **Üstün, A., et al. (2024). "Aya Model: An Instruction Finetuned Open-Access Multilingual Language Model." ACL 2024. arXiv:2402.07827** [M] — multilingual safety context distillation during instruction tuning. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07827)

**Prompt-/inference-time**
- Wang et al. (XSafety): safety/English-thinking prompts reduce ChatGPT non-English unsafe responses (~halved). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905) [M]
- **Li, H., Xu, J., Cui, G., Guan, C., Mo, F., Huang, K. (2025). "Multilingual Collaborative Defense for Large Language Models." arXiv:2505.11835.** [V] — optimized soft safety prompt (MCD) on multilingual MaliciousInstruct/AdvBench; outperforms baselines incl. zero-shot languages, reduces false refusals; no numbers in abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11835)
- Atil et al. 2025: simple defenses effective but language- and model-dependent. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00689) [V]
- Translate-then-act: snippet (arXiv:2601.08668) translate-then-detoxify cuts false refusals 11.78% -> 1.09% [U]; Shukla et al. conclude translated benchmarks are necessary but insufficient. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07963) [V]

**External guards**
- PolyGuard (+5.5% over baselines), IndicGuard (beats CultureGuard), BanglaVeilGuard (ASR 93.8-100% -> 6.3%; 88.5% unsafe recall; over-refusal cost). — [PolyGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04377v2), [IndicGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22841), [BanglaVeilGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21880) [V]

### Inferences
- Reported gains are mostly on closed models or MultiJail/AdvBench-style translated sets, rarely Hindi specifically and rarely with refusal style as the metric. If the study's own non-mechanistic baselines (e.g., system prompt, translate-to-English) fail to close the Hindi gap, that is consistent with Atil et al.'s "language- and model-dependent" finding and RefusEU's poor-transfer finding, and should be framed that way.
- Guard-based fixes sidestep the model's own refusal behaviour and so do not address the study's question (why the base chat model refuses less).

### Gaps
- No verified primary paper found that evaluates a pure "translate Hindi to English, then answer" pipeline on Llama-3.1-8B or Qwen3-8B; E-Proxy is the closest (training-based English proxy).
- MPO venue, RefusEU arXiv-id/date consistency, and the distillation paper's arXiv id are unverified.
- "Why Do Safety Guardrails Degrade Across Languages?" (arXiv:2605.17173) surfaced only as a search title [U]; worth checking as it may be directly relevant.
