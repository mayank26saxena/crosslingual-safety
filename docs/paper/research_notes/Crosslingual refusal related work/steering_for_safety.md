# Activation Steering for Safety and Refusal: Conditional/Gated Steering and Multilingual Settings (annotated bibliography, 2023-2026)

Scope note. Every metadata line below (authors, title, date, arXiv id, venue comment) was checked against the arXiv API record on 2026-11-15, unless marked **[venue unverified]**. Summaries ("Did/found") come from the arXiv abstract. Where I used more than the abstract (for CAST, the full HTML paper), I say so. "Relation to study" lines compare each entry with this paper's method: a gate reads the projection of the last prompt token onto an English-fitted refusal direction at one layer. Steering fires when the projection exceeds a threshold, set at the 50th percentile of development harmful scores (English-calibrated for Qwen, Hindi-calibrated for Llama). When it fires, a fixed vector is added at a later layer at all positions. The vector is either an explicit-vs-soft refusal direction or the refusal direction itself. Settings are chosen by a pre-registered rule with guards (harmless over-refusal, BROKEN/garbled replies, English refusal, MMLU), and the chosen setting is tested once on held-out items.

## Q1. Foundations: ActAdd, CAA, RepE, ITI (and the refusal direction)

### Takeaway
The study's vectors are difference-in-means directions added to the residual stream, which descends from ActAdd and CAA. The study's refusal direction follows Arditi et al. RepE and ITI are the broader "read a direction, then write along it" framing. None of these five works gates the intervention or looks at non-English prompts.

### Cited Findings
- **Turner, A. M., Thiergart, L., Leech, G., Udell, D., Vazquez, J. J., Mini, U., MacDiarmid, M. (2023). "Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering" (first circulated as "Activation Addition"). arXiv:2308.10248.** [venue unverified; arXiv v5, no venue in the record]
  - Did/found: introduced Activation Addition (ActAdd). A steering vector is computed from the activation difference of one prompt pair (e.g. "Love" minus "Hate") and added during the forward pass to control outputs such as sentiment and topic, with no optimisation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248)
  - Relation to study: the conceptual ancestor of additive steering. The study adds a fixed vector the same way but derives it from many contrastive examples (difference in means) and applies it conditionally rather than unconditionally.
- **Panickssery (Rimsky), N., Gabrieli, N., Schulz, J., Tong, M., Hubinger, E., Turner, A. M. (2023/2024). "Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition." arXiv:2312.06681; ACL 2024.** [the ACL 2024 venue comes from common citation; the arXiv record lists no venue]
  - Did/found: CAA averages residual-stream differences over many positive/negative pairs and adds the vector "at all token positions after the user's prompt" with a positive or negative coefficient. On Llama 2 Chat it changed behaviour on multiple-choice and open-ended tasks and "minimally reduces capabilities." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681)
  - Relation to study: closest methodological template for the study's steering vectors (difference in means, fixed coefficient). Differences: the study adds at all positions including the prompt, CAA's behaviours do not include multilingual refusal, and CAA is not gated.
- **Zou, A., Phan, L., Chen, S., Campbell, J., Guo, P., Ren, R., Pan, A., Yin, X., Mazeika, M., Dombrowski, A.-K., Goel, S., Li, N., Byun, M. J., Wang, Z., Mallen, A., Basart, S., Koyejo, S., Song, D., Fredrikson, M., Kolter, J. Z., Hendrycks, D. (2023). "Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency." arXiv:2310.01405.** [venue unverified; arXiv preprint]
  - Did/found: framed "representation engineering" around population-level representations, with methods for both monitoring (reading) and manipulating (controlling) concepts such as honesty and harmfulness. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405)
  - Relation to study: the study's gate is a RepE-style "reading" step (a projection onto a concept direction) feeding a "control" step. The study makes the read-then-write loop explicit and conditional.
- **Li, K., Patel, O., Viégas, F., Pfister, H., Wattenberg, M. (2023). "Inference-Time Intervention: Eliciting Truthful Answers from a Language Model." arXiv:2306.03341; NeurIPS 2023 (spotlight).**
  - Did/found: shifted activations along probe-derived directions in a small set of attention heads. Alpaca's TruthfulQA truthfulness rose from 32.5% to 65.1%. Reported a truthfulness/helpfulness trade-off that is controlled through intervention strength. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03341)
  - Relation to study: an early instance of choosing intervention strength against a side-effect metric, which parallels the study's guards. ITI targets truthfulness, not refusal, and is always on.
- **Arditi, A., Obeso, O., Syed, A., Paleka, D., Panickssery, N., Gurnee, W., Nanda, N. (2024). "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction." arXiv:2406.11717; NeurIPS 2024.** [the NeurIPS venue comes from common citation; the arXiv record lists none]
  - Did/found: across 13 open chat models up to 72B, ablating one difference-in-means direction stops refusal of harmful instructions, and adding it elicits refusal on harmless ones. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)
  - Relation to study: the source of the "English-fitted refusal direction" used both as the gate's read-out axis and as one of the two candidate steering vectors.

### Inferences
- The study combines two roles that prior foundational work kept separate: the refusal direction serves as a detector (via its projection) and as an actuator (via addition). Arditi et al. used it only causally. RepE discusses both roles but not as a gated pipeline.

### Gaps
- I could not verify peer-reviewed venues for ActAdd or RepE from the arXiv records. Cite them as arXiv preprints unless a venue is confirmed.

## Q2. Conditional / gated steering (CAST and related), and how thresholds are set

### Takeaway
Gating steering on an internal detector is an established idea, introduced for refusal by CAST (ICLR 2025) and anticipated by Stickland et al. (2024) with "classify then steer." Thresholds are usually chosen by grid search that maximises F1 on harmful-vs-harmless training data (CAST), learned by logistic regression (AdaSteer, Aziz et al.), or fixed at zero (ALTSTEER). The study's choices differ: a percentile threshold on development harmful scores, the refusal direction itself as the gate axis, and per-language calibration. The closest prior work is Aziz et al. (2026). They calibrate a gate's threshold with a few target-language examples, and, like the study, they find that Llama benefits from target-language calibration.

### Cited Findings
- **Lee, B. W., Padhi, I., Natesan Ramamurthy, K., Miehling, E., Dognin, P., Nagireddy, M., Dhurandhar, A. (2024). "Programming Refusal with Conditional Activation Steering." arXiv:2409.05907; ICLR 2025 (Spotlight).** IBM; code at github.com/IBM/activation-steering.
  - Did/found: CAST gates a refusal vector on a "condition vector." Rules such as "if input is about hate speech, then refuse" are applied "without requiring weight optimization." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05907)
  - Threshold and evaluation (from the full paper): the condition layer, threshold, and comparison direction are picked by grid search that "maximizes the F1 score" on harmful vs. harmless training data. The condition is checked once on the prompt forward pass, and the behaviour vector is then added at specified layers on every later forward pass. Harmful training prompts were synthetic (about 4,050, 90 per Sorry-Bench category) against Alpaca harmless prompts. Testing used 450 unseen Sorry-Bench prompts and 500 unseen Alpaca prompts. Example (Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat): harmful/harmless refusal of 45.78%/0.00% at base, 100%/96.40% with the unconditional refusal vector, and 90.67%/2.20% with the condition. No multilingual evaluation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05907v3)
  - Relation to study: CAST is the main precedent. Differences: (i) the study's gate reads the refusal direction, not a separately learned condition vector; (ii) the threshold is the median of development harmful scores, not an F1-optimal grid point; (iii) the gate is fitted in English and tested cross-lingually, with language-specific threshold calibration for Llama; (iv) the study adds explicit guards (BROKEN/garbled rate, English-refusal retention, MMLU) and uses a pre-registered selection rule with a single held-out test.
- **Cooper Stickland, A., Lyzhov, A., Pfau, J., Mahdi, S., Bowman, S. R. (2024). "Steering Without Side Effects: Improving Post-Deployment Control of Language Models." arXiv:2406.15518.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: motivates classifying inputs as problematic and steering only those, noting that steering harms performance when the classifier is wrong. Proposes KL-then-steer (KTS), which fine-tunes the model to minimise KL between steered and unsteered outputs on benign inputs. KTS prevented 44% of jailbreaks on Llama-2-chat-7B while keeping MT-Bench helpfulness close to the original. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15518)
  - Relation to study: an early statement of the gated-steering rationale and of the side-effect problem on false positives. The study is training-free and controls false-positive cost through the harmless over-refusal guard, not KL training.
- **Zhao, W., Guo, J., Hu, Y., Deng, Y., Zhang, A., Sui, X., Han, X., Zhao, Y., Qin, B., Chua, T.-S., Liu, T. (2025). "AdaSteer: Your Aligned LLM is Inherently an Adaptive Jailbreak Defender." arXiv:2504.09466; EMNLP 2025 (main).** Venue taken from the ACL Anthology PDF listing 2025.emnlp-main.1248.
  - Did/found: replaces fixed coefficients with input-adaptive ones learned by logistic regression. Steering runs along both a Rejection Direction and a Harmfulness Direction ("R-Law": inputs opposing the rejection direction need stronger steering; "H-Law": harmfulness separates adversarial from benign). Evaluated on LLaMA-3.1, Gemma-2, and Qwen2.5. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09466); [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1248.pdf)
  - Relation to study: soft (continuous) gating, where the study uses a hard threshold. A search-result snippet says AdaSteer's authors flag low-resource languages as an open limitation (unverified; I did not read that section). Aziz et al. (below) report that AdaSteer has high harmless refusal in low-resource languages.
- **Sheng, L., Shen, C., Zhao, W., Fang, J., Liu, X., Liang, Z., Wang, X., Zhang, A., Chua, T.-S. (2025). "AlphaSteer: Learning Refusal Steering with Principled Null-Space Constraint." arXiv:2506.07022; ICLR 2026.**
  - Did/found: learns a transformation that outputs a near-zero steering vector for benign activations (null-space constraint) and a refusal-direction vector for malicious ones (linear regression). It is framed as a principled replacement for "vector calibration and conditional steering." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07022)
  - Relation to study: an implicit, learned gate, where the study uses an explicit thresholded one. English-only, as far as the abstract states.
- **Han, P., Qian, C., Chen, X., Zhang, Y., Ji, H., Zhang, D. (2025). "SafeSwitch: Steering Unsafe LLM Behavior via Internal Activation Signals" (earlier version titled "Internal Activation as the Polar Star for Steering Unsafe LLM Behavior"). arXiv:2502.01042.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: a probe on internal states detects harmful intent and switches on a tuned "safety head" (less than 6% of parameters) only when needed. Reports about 80% fewer harmful outputs with strong utility, plus more informative refusals. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01042)
  - Relation to study: same detect-then-intervene structure, but the actuator is trained parameters, not an added vector.
- **Shen, G., Zhao, D., Dong, Y., He, X., Zeng, Y. (2024). "Jailbreak Antidote: Runtime Safety-Utility Balance via Sparse Representation Adjustment in Large Language Models." arXiv:2410.02298; ICLR 2025.**
  - Did/found: shifts hidden states along a safety direction with adjustable strength. Adjusting about 5% of the state is reported to be as effective as adjusting all of it. Evaluated on 9 LLMs (2B-72B), against 10 jailbreak attacks and 6 defences. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02298)
  - Relation to study: ungated strength-dial steering. A natural baseline the study improves on by gating.
- **Kwon, H., Lim, B., Kim, K., Kim, Y. (2026). "ALTSTEER: Selective Safety Steering for Moving Beyond Hard Refusals to Constructive Alternatives." arXiv:2608.30197; EMNLP 2026 (main).**
  - Did/found: uses an internal refusal-relevant signal to decide when to steer, then staged steering from refusal toward constructive alternatives. Evaluated on Llama-3.1 and Qwen2.5. A search snippet of the paper says the gate threshold is zero alignment with the refusal direction, and that zero gave the best accuracy/F1 among the thresholds tested (not verified in the full text). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30197)
  - Relation to study: the closest English-only analogue of a refusal-direction-projection gate. It also cares about the kind of refusal produced (constructive vs. hard), which parallels the study's explicit-vs-soft refusal vector.
- **Aziz, R., Hanif, I. A., Koto, F. (2026). "Low-Resource Safety Failures Are Action Failures, Not Representation Failures." arXiv:2606.01196.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: across 3 instruction-tuned models and 23 languages, harmful refusal drops from 87.9% (high-resource) to 43.9% (low-resource) while harmless refusal stays low. A harmfulness direction learned on high-resource activations still separates low-resource harmful from harmless prompts, but low-resource scores shift downward. A low-rank logistic classifier trained on high-resource activations, with its threshold calibrated on a few target-language examples, conditionally adds or ablates the high-resource harmfulness direction. With 32 target-language examples per class, mean selectivity (harmful minus harmless refusal) was 54.5, against 33.6 for CAST and 6.8 for AdaSteer, with MMLU preserved. "HRL-only calibration improves selectivity for Qwen and Gemma, whereas Llama benefits from target-language calibration." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01196)
  - Relation to study: **the most directly comparable prior work. Cite it prominently.** Both diagnose a downward score shift that a cross-lingual gate threshold has to absorb. Both find that Llama needs target-language calibration while Qwen does not, which independently matches the study's English threshold for Qwen and Hindi threshold for Llama. Differences: Aziz et al. gate with a learned classifier and steer along a harmfulness direction (add or ablate). The study gates on the raw refusal-direction projection with a percentile threshold, steers along refusal or explicit-vs-soft directions, and adds pre-registration, BROKEN/garbled and English-refusal guards, and a single held-out test.
- **Kasliwal, A., Seth, P., Sankarapu, V. K. (2026). "C-ΔΘ: Circuit-Restricted Weight Arithmetic for Selective Refusal." arXiv:2602.04521.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: distils category-selective refusal into an offline weight edit on an EAP-IG-localised circuit (typically under 5% of parameters), so no inference-time hooks are needed. Compared against activation steering and CAST. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04521)
  - Relation to study: an alternative to inference-time gating. Useful for the discussion of deployment cost.
- **Wang, T., Jiao, X., Zhu, Y., Chen, Z., He, Y., Chu, X., Gao, J., Wang, Y., Ma, L. (2024). "Adaptive Activation Steering: A Tuning-Free LLM Truthfulness Improvement Method for Diverse Hallucinations Categories." arXiv:2406.00034; WWW 2025.**
  - Did/found: ACT adaptively shifts activations toward a "truthful" direction during inference, with steering intensity adapted to the input. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00034)
  - Relation to study: an input-adaptive steering precedent outside safety.
- **Wang, W., Yang, J., Peng, W. (2024). "Semantics-Adaptive Activation Intervention for LLMs via Dynamic Steering Vectors." arXiv:2410.12299.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: SADI builds an input-dependent (dynamic) steering vector instead of a fixed one. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12299)
  - Relation to study: the study deliberately uses a fixed vector plus a binary gate, which is simpler and easier to audit.

### Inferences
- Threshold-setting practice varies widely, and the field has not standardised it. Most papers choose thresholds or coefficients on training/validation data drawn from the same distribution as the test. Only Aziz et al. examine cross-lingual calibration. A pre-registered selection rule with guards and a single held-out test appears to be uncommon in this literature. I found no other paper that states it.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full texts of ALTSTEER, AdaSteer, or SafeSwitch to confirm exact threshold procedures and held-out protocols. The ALTSTEER "zero threshold" detail comes from a search snippet.
- Venues for Stickland et al., SafeSwitch, SADI, C-ΔΘ, and Aziz et al. were not confirmed.

## Q3. Steering refusal specifically: raising/lowering refusal, over-refusal fixes, side effects

### Takeaway
Refusal steering runs in both directions: raising refusal (Jailbreak Antidote, AdaSteer, AlphaSteer, SAE features) and lowering false refusal (SCANS, single-vector ablation). The recurring side effects are over-refusal of benign prompts, capability loss on benchmarks (O'Brien et al.), and safety leakage from unrelated steering vectors. These are usually measured with XSTest/OKTest-style benign sets, MMLU or other benchmarks, and MT-Bench. Garbled or incoherent output is rarely reported as its own metric, so the study's BROKEN guard is a point of difference.

### Cited Findings
- **Cao, Z., Yang, Y., Zhao, H. (2024). "SCANS: Mitigating the Exaggerated Safety for LLMs via Safety-Conscious Activation Steering" (also cited as "Nothing in Excess..."). arXiv:2408.11491; AAAI 2025.**
  - Did/found: extracts refusal steering vectors, uses vocabulary projection to anchor safety-critical layers, and tracks hidden-state transitions to decide the steering direction per input. Reports state of the art on XSTest and OKTest without harming defence or capability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11491)
  - Relation to study: SCANS is also a per-input conditional steer, but it reduces over-refusal where the study increases under-refusal. Its over-refusal benchmarks (XSTest, OKTest) are what the study's harmless guard stands in for.
- **Wang, X., Hu, C., Röttger, P., Plank, B. (2024). "Surgical, Cheap, and Flexible: Mitigating False Refusal in Language Models via Single Vector Ablation." arXiv:2410.03415; ICLR 2025.**
  - Did/found: extracts a "false refusal vector." Ablating it lowers false refusal while keeping safety and general capability, and it allows fine-grained safety calibration. Training-free. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03415)
  - Relation to study: shows that refusal sub-directions (true vs. false refusal) can be separated, which is analogous to the study's explicit-vs-soft refusal direction. The study adds rather than ablates.
- **O'Brien, K., Majercak, D., Fernandes, X., Edgar, R., Bullwinkel, B., Chen, J., Nori, H., Carignan, D., Horvitz, E., Poursabzi-Sangdeh, F. (2024). "Steering Language Model Refusal with Sparse Autoencoders." arXiv:2411.11296.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: amplifying SAE features that mediate refusal improves robustness to single- and multi-turn jailbreaks, but causes "systematic degradation of performance across multiple benchmark tasks, even on safe inputs." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11296)
  - Relation to study: documents the capability cost of always-on refusal steering, which motivates the study's MMLU guard and its gating (steering only fires on gate-positive inputs).
- **Zhao, J., Huang, J., Wu, Z., Bau, D., Shi, W. (2025). "LLMs Encode Harmfulness and Refusal Separately." arXiv:2507.11878.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: a harmfulness direction distinct from the refusal direction exists. Steering along harmfulness changes the model's judgement of harm, while steering along refusal elicits refusal directly. Some jailbreaks lower refusal signals without changing internal harmfulness belief. Proposes "Latent Guard," a detector built on the harmfulness representation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878)
  - Relation to study: an important caveat for the gate design. The study gates on the refusal direction, but this work and Aziz et al. suggest a harmfulness direction might be a more robust detector across languages. Worth discussing as an alternative gate axis.
- **Li, Y., Fastowski, A., Zaradoukas, E., Prenkaj, B., Kasneci, G. (2026). "Analysing the Safety Pitfalls of Steering Vectors." arXiv:2603.24543.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: a safety audit of CAA vectors on JailbreakBench. Steering for unrelated behaviours can raise jailbreak ASR by up to 57% or lower it by up to 50%, which the authors attribute to overlap with refusal directions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24543)
  - Relation to study: supports measuring off-target safety effects. The study's English-refusal guard checks that steering does not damage English behaviour.
- **Wang, H., Shu, K. (2023). "Trojan Activation Attack: Red-Teaming Large Language Models using Activation Steering for Safety-Alignment." arXiv:2311.09433; CIKM 2024.**
  - Did/found: uses activation steering as an attack, injecting vectors to break safety alignment. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09433)
  - Relation to study: the dual-use side. The same mechanism that removes refusal can be gated to restore it.
- **Pan, L., Tong, Y., Zhang, X., Zhang, X., Zhou, J., Chu, Z. (2025). "Understanding and Mitigating Overrefusal in LLMs from an Unveiling Perspective of Safety Decision Boundary." arXiv:2505.18325.** [found via arXiv search; abstract not read in detail; venue unverified]
  - Relation to study: a possible extra citation on over-refusal geometry. Check before citing.

### Inferences
- Side effects are usually measured with (a) benign-refusal benchmarks, (b) multiple-choice capability benchmarks such as MMLU, and (c) chat quality via MT-Bench. Fluency or coherence breakdown (the study's BROKEN category) is seldom a primary reported metric in the abstracts reviewed, so explicitly guarding against garbled output is a contribution worth stating.

### Gaps
- I found no abstract-level evidence of a paper that reports a garbled/incoherent-output rate as a formal guard for refusal steering. A full-text check of SCANS, AdaSteer, and BabelSteering might find one.

## Q4. Steering in multilingual settings

### Takeaway
English-derived refusal directions transfer to other safety-aligned languages (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2025), but transfer is graded for low-resource languages (Ivanova, 2026). In 2026, the first English-vector steering methods aimed at multilingual safety appeared: BabelSteering (always-on; Bengali +17 pp harmful refusal, but pseudo-harmful refusal +13 pp on average) and Aziz et al. (gated, calibrated). Mechanistic studies (Minionese; Kompella & Mahajan) agree that harm is detected across languages but refusal is under-triggered. Language steering vectors for non-safety goals are well established.

### Cited Findings
- **Wang, X., Wang, M., Liu, Y., Schütze, H., Plank, B. (2025). "Refusal Direction is Universal Across Safety-Aligned Languages." arXiv:2505.17306; NeurIPS 2025.** [venue from a later paper's reference list, seen in a search snippet; not confirmed on the NeurIPS site]
  - Did/found: PolyRefuse (14 languages, translated). An English refusal vector bypasses refusal in other languages "with near-perfect effectiveness," and directions from any safety-aligned language transfer. Transfer is attributed to refusal vectors being parallel across languages. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)
  - Relation to study: justifies fitting the refusal direction in English and reading it for Hindi prompts. The study tests the inverse, practical use: adding refusal to repair a cross-lingual gap rather than ablating it.
- **Ivanova, A. (2026). "Is Refusal Direction Universal across Low-Resourced Languages?" arXiv:2610.28897; BlackboxNLP@EMNLP 2026 (Reproducibility Track).**
  - Did/found: extends PolyRefuse to Belarusian, Bashkir, and Tajik on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. "Universality is graded rather than binary." Directions from strongly misaligned languages fail to modulate refusal, and geometric alignment and behavioural transfer are partly dissociated. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28897)
  - Relation to study: geometric alignment does not guarantee behavioural transfer. This supports the study's behavioural guards and per-language threshold calibration.
- **Stein, E. V., Meier, D., Ruas, T., Wahle, J. P., Gipp, B. (2026). "BabelSteering: Multilingual Safety Alignment via English Steering Vectors." arXiv:2608.16577.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: steers along English-derived refusal directions in 8 languages, measuring harmful refusal, over-refusal, and utility together. For Gemma 7B, harmful refusal rises 11 pp on average (Bengali +17 pp) with no Global-MMLU loss, but pseudo-harmful refusal rises 13 pp on average. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16577)
  - Relation to study: **closest multilingual-steering comparator.** The abstract describes the intervention as unconditional, and the over-refusal increase it reports is exactly what the study's gate targets. The study adds gating, a garbled-output guard, pre-registration, and a held-out test.
- **Aziz, Hanif, Koto (2026), arXiv:2606.01196.** See Q2. A gated, threshold-calibrated, cross-lingual harmfulness-direction intervention across 23 languages. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01196)
- **Ifebi, C., Kong, B., Mehrotra, A. (2026). "Minionese: Comprehensive Benchmark and Mechanistic Study of Multilingual LLM Safety." arXiv:2607.10112.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: an 18-language, 4-tier jailbreak benchmark covering translation, code-switching, transliteration, and translationese. Low-resource jailbreaks route harmful content through a subspace that "projects insufficiently onto the refusal directions, leaving the refusal mechanism intact but untriggered." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10112)
  - Relation to study: a mechanistic account consistent with the study's premise, since a low refusal-direction projection is exactly what the gate measures. It also raises a tension: if Hindi harmful prompts project weakly, an English-calibrated threshold may under-fire, which is the motivation for Hindi calibration on Llama.
- **Kompella, R. P., Mahajan, A. (2026). "Decided Upstream, Written Late: Locating and Pricing the Cross-Lingual Refusal Circuit of a Multilingual MoE." arXiv:2608.08032; Actionable Interpretability workshop @ COLM 2026.**
  - Did/found: in Sarvam (an Indic MoE), the harm direction is nearly language-invariant mid-network (English-vs-Indic cosine ≈0.9 at L11), and upstream steering causally controls refusal. However, the detection direction is orthogonal to the late, generation-time "write" of refusal. Prices different interventions on the writer circuit. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08032)
  - Relation to study: Indic-language evidence that detection (gate) and actuation (steer) live in different places. This fits the study's design of reading at one layer and steering at a later one.
- **Yoon, C., Park, J., Ritter, A. (2026). "Who Pays More for Safety? Measuring the Disparate Cost of Safety Alignment across Languages." arXiv:2608.22490; EMNLP 2026 (main).**
  - Did/found: non-English users bear a higher "Safety Cost" (utility loss from alignment), and some languages face a "double penalty": weaker protection and larger utility loss. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22490)
  - Relation to study: motivates guarding against over-refusal in the target language, not just in English.
- **Deng, Y., Zhang, W., Pan, S. J., Bing, L. (2023). "Multilingual Jailbreak Challenges in Large Language Models." arXiv:2310.06474; ICLR 2024.** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06474)
  - Did/found: documents unintentional (non-English user) and intentional (attack) multilingual jailbreaks. Relation: a background citation for the cross-lingual safety gap.
- **Shen, L., Tan, W., Chen, S., Chen, Y., Zhang, J., Xu, H., Zheng, B., Koehn, P., Khashabi, D. (2024). "The Language Barrier: Dissecting Safety Challenges of LLMs in Multilingual Contexts." arXiv:2401.13136.** [venue unverified] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13136)
  - Did/found: lower-resource prompts get more unsafe and more irrelevant responses, and safety tuning in low-resource languages helps little, pointing to a pretraining bottleneck. Relation: the "irrelevant responses" finding relates to the study's BROKEN/garbled category.
- **Language steering vectors (non-safety):**
  - Tang, T., Luo, W., Huang, H., Zhang, D., Wang, X., Zhao, X., Wei, F., Wen, J.-R. (2024). ## Q5. Reliability critiques and held-out evaluation

### Takeaway
Several works show that steering effects vary across inputs, sometimes reverse direction, and are sensitive to prompt format, model family, and layer/strength choices. Benchmarks also find that prompting often beats steering. Together these motivate the study's pre-registered selection on development items and single held-out test.

### Cited Findings
- **Tan, D., Chanin, D., Lynch, A., Kanoulas, D., Paige, B., Garriga-Alonso, A., Kirk, R. (2024). "Analyzing the Generalization and Reliability of Steering Vectors." arXiv:2407.12404; NeurIPS 2024.** [the NeurIPS venue comes from common citation; the arXiv record (v8) lists none]
  - Did/found: in-distribution, "steerability is highly variable across different inputs," and spurious biases contribute substantially. Out-of-distribution, vectors often generalise but are brittle to reasonable prompt changes for several concepts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12404)
  - Relation to study: the canonical reliability critique. Cross-lingual transfer is a distribution shift of exactly this kind, and the held-out test addresses it.
- **Braun, J., Eickhoff, C., Krueger, D., Bahrainian, S. A., Krasheninnikov, D. (2025). "Understanding (Un)Reliability of Steering Vectors in Language Models." arXiv:2505.22637; ICLR 2025 Workshop on Foundation Models in the Wild.**
  - Did/found: all 7 prompt types give a net positive effect but high variance, often in the opposite of the desired direction. Higher cosine similarity among training activation differences, and better positive/negative separation along the direction, both predict reliability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22637)
  - Extended in Braun, J. (2026). "Understanding Unreliability of Steering Vectors in Language Models: Geometric Predictors and the Limits of Linear Approximations." arXiv:2602.17881 (MSc thesis, Tübingen). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17881)
  - Relation to study: suggests a diagnostic the study could report, namely the separation of harmful and harmless dev items along the refusal direction per language.
- **Da Silva, P. Q., Sethuraman, H., Rajagopal, D., Hajishirzi, H., Kumar, S. (2025). "Steering off Course: Reliability Challenges in Steering Language Models." arXiv:2504.04635.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: tests DoLa, function vectors, and task vectors on up to 36 models from 14 families (1.5B-70B). Many models show no improvement, and some show degradation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04635)
  - Relation to study: supports reporting per-model results (Qwen vs. Llama behave differently) rather than pooling.
- **Wu, Z., Arora, A., Geiger, A., Wang, Z., Huang, J., Jurafsky, D., Manning, C. D., Potts, C. (2025). "AxBench: Steering LLMs? Even Simple Baselines Outperform Sparse Autoencoders." arXiv:2501.17148; ICML 2025.** [the ICML venue comes from common citation; the arXiv record lists none]
  - Did/found: on Gemma-2-2B and 9B, prompting outperforms all steering methods, followed by fine-tuning. For concept detection, difference-in-means is best, and SAEs are not competitive. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)
  - Relation to study: (i) supports using difference-in-means as the gate's detector; (ii) a reviewer may ask for a prompting baseline, such as a system prompt instructing refusal in Hindi.
- **Im, S., Li, S. (2025). "A Unified Understanding and Evaluation of Steering Methods." arXiv:2502.02716.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: a unified framework and evaluation of steering methods on multiple-choice and open-ended generation. Reports that some methods outperform others (the abstract does not name which; the full text was not read). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02716)
- **Niranjan, C., Jaidka, K., Yeo, G. C. (2025). "On the Limitations of Steering in Language Model Alignment." arXiv:2505.01162.** [venue unverified]
  - Did/found: steering vectors suit specific tasks such as value alignment, but "may not provide a robust foundation for general-purpose alignment," especially in complex scenarios. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01162)
- **Li, J., Li, Y., Huang, K.-H. (2026). "Steering Vector Fields for Context-Aware Inference-Time Control in Large Language Models." arXiv:2602.01654; EMNLP 2026 (main).**
  - Did/found: argues that a static vector assumes a constant improving direction, and fails when the locally effective direction varies with activation, giving weak or reversed effects. Proposes context-dependent gradient-field steering. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01654)
  - Relation to study: a theoretical reason why a fixed vector might work for one language or model and not another, and a reason to keep the claims narrow.

### Inferences
- In the abstracts reviewed, held-out evaluation in the steering literature mostly means unseen prompts from the same distribution (as in CAST's unseen Sorry-Bench and Alpaca items). Explicit pre-registration of the selection rule before a one-shot held-out test is not described in any of them. This is a methodological contribution of the study, but abstract-level reading cannot rule out that someone has done it.

### Gaps
- No full-text check was done on whether any multilingual steering paper (BabelSteering, Aziz et al.) separates development-set selection from test evaluation. Verify before claiming novelty on protocol.
- Exact NeurIPS/ICML/ACL venue confirmations for Tan et al., Arditi et al., AxBench, CAA, and Wang et al. (2025) rely on common citation, not on the arXiv record. Confirm on the proceedings sites when finalising the bibliography.
