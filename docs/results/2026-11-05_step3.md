# Step 3 results: semantic harm concept or language-dependent refusal pathway

Pre-registration: [2026-11-05_semantic_vs_pathway.md](../prereg/2026-11-05_semantic_vs_pathway.md). Aggregate outputs are in
`results/step3/`, `results/step3_gemma/` and `results/step3_llama/`. Raw replies are not tracked.

## Summary

The hypothesis is **partly supported and not established**.

- **Shared representation of intent:** supported in Chinese, Arabic and Bengali on all three models. In Swahili it holds on Gemma
  only.
- **Weaker link from that representation into refusal:** not established. The registered robustness check fails in all 12
  language-model pairs at the primary layers.
- **Shared refusal mechanism:** not established. On Qwen, removing an English direction lowers refusal in Chinese, Arabic and
  Bengali, but the judge gate misses narrowly, so this is provisional. On Gemma and Llama the registered direction fails its English
  positive control, so those results cannot support or count against a shared mechanism.
- **Exploratory:** a direction chosen on half A does lower refusal on Llama in English, Chinese and Bengali under a passing judge
  gate. It does not replace a registered verdict.

## Limits that apply throughout

- Steps 1 and 2 stand. Gate E1 failed and the probe test was NO-GO, so no safety-fix claim is made and no steering fix was tested.
- The outcome is explicit refusal as graded by language models, with no human audit. No native reader reviewed any language.
- XSTest translations in Chinese, Arabic and Swahili come from Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, and Bengali from Claude Opus 5.5. Translator and
  language are confounded. Arabic has 173 format-passing test items in S3.
- These are three open models and five languages. Direction removal tests only the direction removed.

Qwen and Gemma language-level readings are therefore provisional. Only Llama has a trustworthy S2 reading, and its removal positive
control fails.

Swahili on Qwen is at the floor (2.6%), so it cannot test removal, and judge agreement there is near chance (kappa 0.09) with 96% raw
agreement.

**Addition is not a clean control of harm-related refusal.** It also raises refusal on harmless prompts. Examples: Llama English
11.5% → 64.6%, Gemma Bengali 21.5% → 83.8%, Qwen Bengali 7.7% → 28.5%.

**Damage check:** the repeated-text detector flags nothing in any condition reported here, so this check has little sensitivity. On the
exploratory test below, a stricter repeated-phrase check flags at most 2.6%. Latin-script share rises little under registered removal. It is uninformative for Swahili, where a
rough English word count on Qwen shows replies staying non-English.

## Exploratory: direction search on Gemma and Llama

Added after the registered failure, by dated addendum and before the search ran. Twelve candidates per model were fitted on English
half A and selected on English half A. The registered last-token candidates did not lower refusal there. Both selected directions use
the post-instruction token mean: Gemma layer 19 and Llama layer 14.

Harmful half B, explicit refusal, unchanged → selected direction removed:

| Language | Gemma | Beats both controls | Llama | Beats both controls |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| English | 80.3 → 39.5 | Yes | 75.0 → 55.3 | Yes |
| Chinese | 65.8 → 48.7 | Yes | 64.5 → 52.6 | Yes |
| Arabic | 72.4 → 39.5 | Yes | 61.8 → 57.9 | No |
| Bengali | 69.7 → 26.3 | Yes | 27.6 → 9.2 | Yes |
| Swahili | 61.8 → 25.0 | Yes | 11.8 → 7.9 | No |

Judge agreement on these replies: Gemma fails (English kappa 0.306, non-English 0.437), and Llama passes (0.817 and 0.861).

On Gemma, removal also raises the Latin-script share of replies: Chinese 24% to 50%, Arabic 4% to 40%, Bengali 4% to 21%. Part of the
Gemma fall may be a switch into another language rather than a change in refusal. The second judge also finds much lower refusal rates
than the main judge on Gemma's replies, for example English 10.5% against 39.5%.

The direction search compares candidates on the items they were fitted on, then tests on separate items. The selected direction also
differs from the one used on Qwen, so the three models are not directly comparable here.

## Corrections made during analysis

- Three unparsed Qwen grades and two unparsed Gemma grades crashed the report. They are left out of kappa and counted as not an
  explicit refusal in the rates.
- `step3.py report` does not apply Holm. `step3_verify.py` does, and recomputes every rate from graded replies without a mismatch.
  Holm changed one Qwen verdict: Bengali addition on harmful prompts, with corrected p = 0.06 against the matched control.

## What would settle more

- A Qwen run of the post-instruction direction, so all three models are compared on the same definition.
- A confirmatory, pre-registered test of the selected directions on new items.
- Native review of the translations, and a stronger judge on the Qwen and Gemma replies.
